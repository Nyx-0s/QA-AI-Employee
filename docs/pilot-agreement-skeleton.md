# Pilot & retainer agreement — working skeleton

*Plain-language skeleton for the free 7-day pilot and the monthly retainer.
**Not legal advice** — have a professional review before first signature.
Everything here mirrors what the site and offer sheet already promise; the
contract's job is to say the same thing with no surprises.*

---

## 1 · Parties & scope

- **Provider:** ConsentArk (operator), c0nsen7.xyz.
- **Customer:** the business hiring the AI employee.
- **The employee:** one role from the current catalog (Data Clerk, QA
  Analyst, or Outreach Assistant, with the Scheduler capability as
  applicable), configured for the Customer's real workflow as agreed at
  kickoff and recorded in the job description (Appendix A).

## 2 · The pilot (free, 7 days)

1. Provider configures and deploys the employee on the Customer's appliance
   at no cost. The Customer provides one 30-minute kickoff and the access
   the job description requires — nothing more.
2. The employee works under three standing rules for the entire term:
   **allowlist** (only domains named in Appendix A), **consent gate** (no
   submission, booking, or outbound message without the Customer's
   explicit approval), **audit trail** (every step logged in plain English).
3. The Outreach Assistant produces drafts only; nothing is transmitted by
   the employee at any time.
4. **Ownership during pilot:** all records the employee produces — audit
   trails, reports, drafts — belong to the Customer, during and after,
   whatever they decide.
5. **End of pilot:** the Customer decides to convert (§3) or stop (§4).
   Silence is not conversion.

## 3 · The retainer (after conversion)

- **Setup fee** (per role, per the signed rate card) invoiced at conversion.
- **Monthly retainer** invoiced monthly in advance; cancel any time; no
  contract term; no early-termination fee.
- **Rate lock:** any founding or promotional terms are honored for the
  stated period (e.g., 12 months) in writing in Appendix B.
- **What the retainer includes:** operation and supervision of the
  employee, corrective maintenance, gate review support, and monthly
  evidence summary from the audit trail.

## 4 · The guarantee (30-day money-back)

- Within 30 days of conversion, if the Customer concludes the employee does
  not clearly save them hours, Provider refunds every dollar paid — setup
  and retainer — on request, no forms.
- The Customer's audit trail is attached to the refund as the shared record
  of what was (or wasn't) saved. Both parties keep it.

## 5 · Data, appliance, and offboarding

- The employee runs in a sealed appliance on hardware the Customer controls;
  one container, one data volume, one customer.
- **The data volume is the Customer's property.** Provider copies it before
  any migration and hands the volume (or a complete export) to the Customer
  at offboarding.
- Provider retains no copy of Customer data after offboarding and confirms
  the deletion in writing.
- Provider never uses Customer data to train models, benchmark, or market.

## 6 · Security & conduct of the employee

- The allowlist and consent gate are structural, not contractual promises:
  they are enforced by the appliance at run time.
- Provider personnel access the appliance only for supervision/maintenance,
  only with the Customer's knowledge, and only through logged channels —
  which are part of the audit trail.
- If the employee makes an error, the audit trail is the shared record;
  root cause and fix are delivered to the Customer in plain English.

## 7 · Liability (skeleton)

- Provider is liable for direct damages up to fees paid in the prior 3
  months. No indemnity for the Customer's own business outcomes; the
  employee advises and executes within its job description, the Customer
  approves what leaves the gate.
- Nothing limits liability for gross negligence or willful misconduct.

## 8 · Term, changes, disputes

- Either party may end the retainer with notice; pilots end by decision or
  at day 7, whichever first.
- Changes to the job description are written into Appendix A (a
  conversation, not a new contract).
- Disputes first go to the audit trail: the parties read the record
  together. Good faith is the default; arbitration venue per operator's
  jurisdiction (confirm with counsel).
